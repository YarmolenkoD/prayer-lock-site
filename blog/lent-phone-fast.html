How to do a phone fast for Lent

By Daniil Yarmolenko · Updated November 2026 · Lent 2027 edition

Fasting from food has always been part of Lent. For many of us the thing that most crowds out God today is not bread but the phone. A 40-day phone fast is a simple, concrete way to make room for prayer between Ash Wednesday and Easter.

Lent 2027 dates: Ash Wednesday is February 10, 2027; Easter Sunday is March 28, 2027. Holy Week runs March 21–27.

Why fast from your phone at all?

Jesus fasted forty days in the wilderness before his ministry began (Matthew 4:1–2). The point of fasting was never the hunger itself; it was turning the heart back to God (Joel 2:12–13). Paul's warning fits our screens well: “all things are lawful unto me, but I will not be brought under the power of any” (1 Corinthians 6:12, KJV).

A phone fast works the same way. You notice how often your hand reaches for the screen, and each time you can turn that reflex into a short prayer instead.

Step 1: Decide exactly what you're giving up

Vague fasts fail. Write your rule down before Ash Wednesday. Pick one of these:

  • App fast: no social media (Instagram, TikTok, X, YouTube Shorts) for 40 days. Calls, messages, maps and work apps stay.
  • Time fast: no phone for the first hour after waking and the last hour before sleep.
  • Prayer-first fast: distracting apps stay locked every day until you have prayed. The rest of the day is normal.
  • Full fast: a basic phone or phone switched off outside of necessary calls. Hard; best for people with a clear reason and family support.

If you've never fasted from your phone, start with the app fast or the prayer-first fast. You can tighten it in Holy Week.

Step 2: Replace the habit, don't just remove it

The empty minutes are the danger. Give them a destination before the fast starts:

  • A fixed daily prayer time, same place and same time every day. Ten minutes is enough to begin.
  • A Lent reading plan: one Gospel through Lent (Mark is 16 chapters, about three chapters a week) or a Psalm a day.
  • A paper journal by your bed instead of the phone.
  • One person a week you call or visit instead of scrolling past their posts.

Step 3: Make the phone help you keep the fast

Willpower alone rarely lasts 40 days. Use the tools already on your phone:

  1. Delete or hide the apps. Move the ones you're fasting from off the home screen, or delete them until Easter.
  2. Use Screen Time limits. On iPhone, Settings → Screen Time → App Limits lets you set a daily limit of a minute on the apps you're giving up. Ask a spouse or friend to set the passcode.
  3. Charge the phone outside the bedroom. A cheap alarm clock removes the main reason to keep it by your bed.
  4. Lock apps until you pray. Prayer Lock blocks the apps you choose during a daily prayer time until you tap “I Prayed”, so the fast always starts with prayer rather than a feed.

Step 4: Plan for work, family and church

  • Tell people. A short note (“I'm off social media for Lent, text or call me”) prevents most problems.
  • Keep work separate. If you need Slack or email for work, fast from them only outside working hours.
  • Fast together. Families, small groups and youth groups keep fasts far better than individuals. Agree on one shared rule and check in weekly.
  • Sundays. Sundays are traditionally feast days and are not counted in the 40. Decide before you start whether you relax the fast on Sundays, and keep that rule.

Step 5: When you slip

You will open an app out of habit. Close it, say a one-line prayer (“Lord, you first”), and carry on. Jesus taught that fasting is between you and the Father (Matthew 6:16–18). It isn't a performance, and one slip doesn't cancel it.

A simple 40-day plan

  • Week 1 (Feb 10–): remove the apps, set your daily prayer time, notice every reflex to reach for the phone.
  • Weeks 2–3: add the reading plan. Most people find the urge to check fades here.
  • Weeks 4–5: extend the prayer time by five minutes and add one phone-free meal a day.
  • Holy Week (Mar 21–27): tighten the fast; consider a full phone-free Good Friday.
  • Easter (Mar 28): celebrate, then decide what to keep.

After Easter: keep what helped

The best outcome of a Lent phone fast is not 40 clean days; it's a new default. Most people keep one thing: no phone before prayer, apps off the home screen, or the phone charging outside the bedroom. Pick yours on Easter Monday.

Frequently asked questions

When does Lent 2027 start and end?

Lent 2027 begins on Ash Wednesday, February 10, 2027, and ends with Holy Week; Easter Sunday is March 28, 2027. The 40 days traditionally exclude Sundays.

Do I have to give up my phone completely for Lent?

No. Most people fast from specific apps (social media, news, games) or from specific times of day, not from calls and messages. A fast you can keep for 40 days is better than a total ban you break on day three.

Are Sundays part of a Lent phone fast?

Traditionally Sundays are feast days and are not counted in the 40 days, so some people relax the fast on Sundays. Decide before Ash Wednesday and keep the same rule all six weeks.

What should I do with the time I save?

Give it a specific place: a fixed daily prayer time, a Bible reading plan, a walk, or a call to someone. Fasting without replacing the habit usually ends in scrolling something else.
