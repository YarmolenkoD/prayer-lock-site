Prayer Lock vs PausePray: which Christian app blocker is right for you?

A PausePray alternative · By Daniil Yarmolenko · Updated November 2026

PausePray pauses a distracting app and guides you through a prayer before it opens; Prayer Lock locks apps for a scheduled prayer session. Both block distracting apps on iPhone through Apple Screen Time, but they answer a different question: do you want a prayer pause every time you open an app, or one protected prayer time each day? Here's an honest comparison.

The core difference

PausePray: Prayer First by Thomas Lee pauses the distracting apps you choose. When you try to open one, it steps in with a guided prayer, optionally read aloud with text-to-speech, and after you finish the app unlocks for a duration you set. It keeps a prayer history and journal. It is free to download with in-app purchases and a 3-day free trial, and as a new app it has very few ratings as of November 2026.

Prayer Lock protects one dedicated prayer time a day. You schedule it, Focus Mode blocks the apps you chose (social media, news, games), and they stay blocked until you finish and tap “I Prayed”. The rest of the day your phone works normally. A daily streak, prayer history and mood tracking show the habit forming over weeks. Blocking runs on-device through Apple's Screen Time API; Prayer Lock never reads the content of your prayers.

When PausePray is the better choice

If you'd like someone to lead the prayer, read aloud, every time you reach for a distracting app, PausePray's guided per-open model is a thoughtful version of that.

When Prayer Lock is the better choice

If you'd rather pray in your own words at a time you set, and simply have your phone stop competing for that time, Prayer Lock is the better fit. It locks your apps for the session, you end it with “I Prayed”, and your streak and history keep you honest.

Frequently asked questions

Does PausePray block apps for a whole prayer session?
No. PausePray pauses an app at the moment you open it and unlocks it after the guided prayer. A dedicated, scheduled prayer session is Prayer Lock's model.

Which has a free trial?
Both. Prayer Lock's yearly plan ($39.99 in the US) comes with a 7-day free trial; PausePray's listing mentions a 3-day free trial.

Can I use Prayer Lock and PausePray together?
Both rely on Apple Screen Time shields, so overlapping rules can conflict. Choose one approach: per-open guided prayers (PausePray) or a protected daily session (Prayer Lock).
